Why this matters for regulated financial entities in India

Statements, circulars, product terms and forms are PDFs, and PDFs are where accessibility most often fails. This summary is written as of 23 November 2026 from public sources. It is not legal advice, so confirm each item on the regulator's own site.

  • The law. Section 40 of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 requires accessibility standards, including for information and communication. In Rajive Raturi v. Union of India (Supreme Court, 8 November 2024) the Court held that the framework must be enforceable and referred to Indian standard IS 17802 (Parts 1 and 2) for ICT.
  • Banking. The Department of Financial Services, Ministry of Finance notified "Accessibility Standards and Guidelines for Banking Sector" on 2 February 2024. Summaries say it covers accessible digital documents such as e-statements. The Reserve Bank of India's Digital Banking Channels Authorisation Directions, 2025 (issued 28 November 2025, effective 1 January 2026) cross-refer to these standards. Check the exact wording on rbi.org.in and the DFS site.
  • The 14 August 2025 RBI circular on digital access (issued after the Supreme Court's order of 30 April 2025 in Pragya Prasun) appears on RBI's lists of withdrawn circulars. Its substance may now sit in the consolidated 2025 directions, so check its current status before citing it.
  • If you are also SEBI-regulated. SEBI's circular of 31 July 2025 is reported to cover tagged PDFs and to require an audit by IAAP-certified professionals that includes usability testing by people with disabilities.

Regulators do not publish a "PDF test". In practice entities are expected to meet WCAG 2.1 AA (through IS 17802 and EN 301 549 clause 10). For PDFs the usual route is a tagged PDF that conforms to PDF/UA, checked against the Matterhorn Protocol, which defines 136 failure conditions of which 87 can be machine-checked and 47 need a person (version 1.02; later versions report slightly different counts).

How to use the result as audit evidence

  1. Test one PDF per template or document type, not every customer copy.
  2. Fix the source or generator, then re-run until nothing under "Needs fixing" is left.
  3. Work through the manual checklist, including a screen-reader pass and review by people with disabilities.
  4. Download the evidence file. It records the file's SHA-256, the date, the tool version, every result and your checklist, so you can show what was checked and when.
  5. Validate independently with veraPDF or PAC, then hand the lot to your accredited auditor.
