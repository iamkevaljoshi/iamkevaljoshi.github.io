More settings

Runs entirely in your browser with its built-in encoders. Images are never uploaded. Metadata such as GPS location is removed.

    Compare

    Original Optimised

    Drag the slider: optimised on the left, original on the right. Use "Actual size" to check fine detail.

    What it does

    Resize

    Most images are uploaded far bigger than they're displayed. A 4000 px phone photo shown at 960 px wastes over 90% of its bytes. Pick the largest size the image is shown at, or twice that for sharp screens.

    Convert and compress

    WebP and AVIF are typically 25–50% smaller than JPEG at the same visual quality. "Smallest" tries every format your browser can create and keeps the winner, avoiding JPEG for images with transparency.

    Responsive sizes

    Creates 480, 960, 1440 and 1920 px versions plus a ready-to-paste srcset, so phones download a small file and large screens a sharp one. Width and height are included to prevent layout shift.

    Why it matters

    • Images are usually the largest thing on a page, and often the Largest Contentful Paint element, so they decide your Core Web Vitals score more than anything else.
    • Always set width and height (the snippet does), so the browser reserves space and the page doesn't jump: that's your Cumulative Layout Shift.
    • Lazy-load images below the fold, never the hero. The snippet uses loading="lazy". Remove it, and add fetchpriority="high", for the main image at the top of the page.
    • Write real alt text. Describe what the image shows and why it's there, or use an empty alt="" if it's purely decorative.
